# The Constitution Coding Manifest
## — A Declaration of Order for Software Engineering in the Age of AI

---

### I. Why We Are Here

AI can write code.

This is not a prophecy of the future. It is the reality of the present.

But amid the cheers of "AI can write code," we witness a dangerous drift: code grows while order collapses; output accelerates while debt piles up. Developers have gone from "the people who write code" to "the people who fix AI's code" — from creators to correctors.

We refuse this future.

We refuse to hand the dignity of engineering over to AI's free play.
We refuse to replace "architecture" with "vibes," and "standards" with "feelings."
We refuse to let "fast" become an excuse for "broken."

Today, we propose a new paradigm.

It belongs to no company, is bound to no model, and depends on no platform.

It belongs only to those creators willing to bear final responsibility for their own code.

---

### II. Core Tenets

**Tenet I: Humans legislate; AI executes.**

AI is not our employee, not our partner, not our co-pilot.

AI is our extension — a reasoning engine that understands rules and executes instructions.

Our role is not "telling AI what to do" but "defining what AI cannot do."
Our value is not "how many lines of code we typed" but "how many effective constraints we enacted."

The legislator sets the boundaries; the executor acts within them.

**Tenet II: The Constitution outranks the conversation.**

The result of a conversation dissolves when its context window closes.
The effect of a constitution persists as long as the project lives.

We do not guarantee quality by "how well we chatted with AI this time."
We guarantee quality by whether the charter is clear, executable, and audited.

What is chatted is "a feeling"; what is written into the constitution is "a rule."

**Tenet III: Constraints are assets, not burdens.**

"Writing specs is too troublesome" — that is the complaint of an old era.
"Amend the spec and AI will fix every violating line of code at once" — that is the leverage of the new one.

One precise constraint file outweighs ten thousand manual code reviews.
One amendment to the charter triggers automatic compliance across every AI output.

Constraints are not shackles that limit creativity — they are the mold that scales it.

**Tenet IV: Open source is independence.**

We do not entrust engineering sovereignty to any closed-source ecosystem.
We do not bind our methodology to any particular model.

OpenCode is our administrative arm; domestic models are our executing officials.
Both are replaceable. The one thing that never changes is the constitution in our hands.

Tools may change, models may change; the constitution never dies, and the system lives forever.

---

### III. Program of Action

We call upon every developer who recognizes this paradigm to follow these principles in practice:

**I. Legislate first, code later.**

Before any line of code is generated by AI, codify your architectural intent, naming conventions, interface contracts, and error-handling patterns into a charter file that AI can read.

**II. Constrain with the charter, not with prompt patches.**

When AI's output falls short, do not revise the prompt — revise the charter.
Do not fix one bug; fix a class of bugs.
Do not correct one violation; eliminate a class of violations.

**III. Audit is both the end and the beginning of the loop.**

Every AI output must pass a compliance audit.
Every audit finding must feed back into the next revision of the charter.

Legislate → Execute → Audit → Amend. The loop never stops; order endures.

**IV. Spread the paradigm, not the tool.**

We share a methodology, not a cracked IDE.
We build a community, not a user base for some platform.
We pursue the return of engineering sovereignty, not the deepening of tool dependency.

**V. Mechanize constraints — do not leave them in words.**

A rule written into a prompt depends on the model's self-discipline; a rule written into a gate depends on the machine's enforcement.
Soft constraints belong to charters and skills; hard constraints belong to gates and plugins.
Every bottom line that can be determined programmatically — the test gate, data backup, secret leakage — must be intercepted by the machine, not left to the model's memory.

**VI. Every R&D effort must give back to the assets.**

The interfaces, tools, and process guides produced are not one-time consumables; they are the addressable assets of the next R&D effort.
They must be distilled into documentation and registered in the resource map; accumulation distinguishes "new" from "update," and updates must not destroy existing content.
Legislate → Execute → Audit → Amend → Accumulate. The loop never stops; the system grows itself.

### IV. Conclusion

This is an age in which AI can generate anything.

Generated code can be garbage — or it can be the foundation stone.

The difference lies not in how strong the AI is, but in whether you have a constitution.

Vibe programming drifts with the current; charter programming legislates the order.

The vibe programmer asks: "AI, what can you do?"
The charter programmer says: "AI, within my rules, this is all you may do."

We choose the latter.

Not because the path is easy, but because it leads to true control.

We are not the "code monkeys" of the AI age.
We are the legislators of the AI age.

In the hetu ecosystem, the legislator has taken his seat — and his name is Hammurabi.
